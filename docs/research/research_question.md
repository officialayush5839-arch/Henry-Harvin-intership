# Research Questions

The research questions for this study have been formulated using the PICO (Population, Intervention, Comparator, Outcome) framework to ensure clarity, focus, and methodological rigor.

## PICO Framework

-   **Population (P):** Patients included in the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS) database with adverse event reports associated with the use of antidiabetic medications between 2018 and 2025.
-   **Intervention/Exposure (I):** Semaglutide, encompassing all approved formulations (Ozempic, Wegovy, Rybelsus).
-   **Comparator (C):** Sodium-glucose cotransporter-2 (SGLT2) inhibitors (e.g., Empagliflozin, Dapagliflozin, Canagliflozin), serving as an active comparator class of antidiabetic medications.
-   **Outcome (O):** Disproportionately higher reporting of psychiatric adverse events (e.g., depression, anxiety, suicidal ideation) categorized under the relevant Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA) System Organ Class (SOC) for Psychiatric Disorders.

## Primary Research Question

Based on the PICO framework, the primary investigation is defined as follows:

**Is Semaglutide associated with a disproportionately higher reporting rate of psychiatric adverse events compared to SGLT2 inhibitors in the FDA FAERS database (2018-2025)?**

## Secondary Research Questions

To provide a more comprehensive analysis of the potential safety signal, the following secondary questions will also be addressed:

1.  **Specific ADR Identification:** What are the most frequently reported specific psychiatric adverse drug reactions (at the MedDRA Preferred Term level) associated with Semaglutide?
2.  **Temporal Trends:** Are there observable temporal trends in the reporting of these psychiatric adverse events? Has the signal strength changed over time as prescribing indications expanded (e.g., post-Wegovy approval)?
3.  **Route of Administration:** Do the reporting patterns of psychiatric adverse events differ significantly by the route of administration (i.e., oral Rybelsus versus subcutaneous Ozempic/Wegovy)?
