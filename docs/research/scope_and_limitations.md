# Scope and Limitations

This document outlines the boundaries of the proposed research on Semaglutide and psychiatric adverse drug reactions (ADRs), defining what is included in the analysis and acknowledging inherent methodological limitations.

## Scope of the Research

### In Scope
The research activities will be confined to the following areas:
-   **FAERS-based Observational Pharmacovigilance:** The primary data source will be the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS), utilizing publicly available quarterly data extracts.
-   **Disproportionality Analysis:** Application of established statistical methods (e.g., Reporting Odds Ratio) to identify potential signals of disproportionate reporting for psychiatric ADRs.
-   **Literature Review:** A comprehensive review of existing literature regarding Semaglutide, GLP-1 receptor agonists, and psychiatric adverse events to contextualize findings.
-   **Simulated Experimental Design:** The creation of a rigorous, structured analytical plan suitable for analyzing retrospective pharmacovigilance data.

### Out of Scope
The following activities are explicitly excluded from this research project:
-   **Actual Clinical Trials:** Conducting new prospective randomized controlled trials (RCTs) or observational clinical studies involving patients.
-   **Molecular Mechanism Investigation:** In vitro or in vivo laboratory experiments to determine the biological or neurochemical pathways of Semaglutide in the central nervous system.
-   **Health Economics:** Cost-benefit analyses or evaluations of the economic impact of Semaglutide-related ADRs.
-   **Individual Patient Data Analysis:** Accessing or analyzing electronic health records (EHRs) or identifiable patient data beyond the de-identified summaries provided in FAERS.

## Limitations

The proposed research relies on spontaneous reporting data, which inherently carries several significant limitations that must be considered when interpreting the results:

1.  **Reporting Bias:**
    *   *Weber Effect:* A recognized phenomenon where reporting rates for a new drug peak within the first few years after approval and then decline, regardless of the actual incidence of the ADR.
    *   *Notoriety Bias (Stimulated Reporting):* Media attention and regulatory investigations (such as the current FDA probe into Semaglutide and suicidal ideation) can artificially inflate the reporting rates for specific adverse events.
2.  **Voluntary Reporting (Underreporting):** FAERS relies on voluntary submissions from healthcare professionals, consumers, and manufacturers. Therefore, it captures only a fraction of the actual adverse events occurring in the population (underreporting).
3.  **Inability to Establish Causation:** Disproportionality analysis of FAERS data can only generate *signals* of association; it cannot definitively prove causality. Confounding factors (e.g., underlying disease state, concomitant medications) cannot be fully controlled.
4.  **Lack of Denominator Data:** FAERS provides the numerator (number of reported events) but lacks the true denominator (total number of patients exposed to the drug). This makes it impossible to calculate true incidence rates or risk.
5.  **Duplicate Reports:** Despite data cleaning efforts, the FAERS database may contain duplicate reports for the same patient event, potentially skewing the analysis.
6.  **Comparator Selection Bias:** The choice of comparator (e.g., SGLT2 inhibitors) can influence the results. Differences in the patient populations prescribed different drug classes (confounding by indication) must be carefully considered.
