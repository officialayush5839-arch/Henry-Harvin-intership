# Research Problem Statement

## Background

Semaglutide, a glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist, has experienced unprecedented clinical uptake in recent years. Initially approved for the management of type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) (Ozempic, 2017), its indications were later expanded to include chronic weight management in adults with obesity or overweight (Wegovy, 2021). The drug's efficacy has led to widespread off-label use and a significant surge in prescriptions. Importantly, GLP-1 receptors are present in the central nervous system (CNS), suggesting that these agents may have direct central effects, which is consistent with their mechanism of promoting satiety.

## The Problem

Despite robust safety data from pre-approval clinical trials, the post-marketing period has seen an emergence of spontaneous reports detailing psychiatric adverse events (AEs) associated with Semaglutide use. These reports include symptoms such as depression, anxiety, and suicidal ideation. Spontaneous reporting systems like the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS) are crucial because randomized controlled trials (RCTs) are often underpowered to detect rare or delayed adverse drug reactions (ADRs) due to limited sample sizes, stringent inclusion/exclusion criteria, and relatively short follow-up periods. Consequently, regulatory bodies, including the FDA, are actively investigating these emerging psychiatric signals.

## Significance

The investigation of these psychiatric signals carries profound significance:
-   **Public Health Impact:** Given the massive and growing patient population utilizing Semaglutide for both diabetes and weight loss, even a rare severe psychiatric ADR could affect a substantial number of individuals.
-   **Regulatory Implications:** Confirmation of these signals could lead to labeling changes, risk evaluation and mitigation strategies (REMS), or altered prescribing guidelines.
-   **Patient Safety:** Ensuring that healthcare providers and patients are fully informed of potential risks is paramount for safe prescribing practices and patient monitoring.

## The Gap

While the emergence of these signals has prompted regulatory scrutiny, there remains a critical gap in the scientific literature. Specifically, systematic disproportionality analyses of psychiatric ADRs that directly compare Semaglutide to other antidiabetic classes (such as SGLT2 inhibitors) are limited. Many existing studies lack active comparators, making it difficult to determine whether the observed psychiatric events are disproportionately associated with Semaglutide itself, or potentially confounded by the underlying conditions (diabetes, obesity) or general medication use. This research aims to address this gap by providing a rigorous comparative analysis using FAERS data.
