# Topic Selection

## Candidate Topics Evaluation

In the process of selecting a research focus, seven potential topics were evaluated against eight critical criteria to ensure feasibility, relevance, and scientific rigor.

### Evaluated Topics
1. AI/ML in Drug Discovery
2. Pharmacovigilance and Adverse Drug Reactions (ADRs)
3. Drug Repurposing
4. Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR)
5. Targeted Drug Delivery and Nanomedicine
6. Biosimilars
7. Personalized Medicine and Pharmacogenomics

### Evaluation Criteria
- **Literature Availability:** Abundance of published studies and methodological references.
- **Public Datasets:** Availability of free, accessible data for analysis.
- **Statistical Analysis Feasibility:** Practicality of applying quantitative methods.
- **Experimental Design Feasibility:** Ability to design a sound simulated experiment.
- **Week 4 Paper Availability:** Availability of relevant, recent papers for reference.
- **Student Feasibility:** Appropriateness for the current skill level and resources.
- **Ethical Feasibility:** Lack of prohibitive ethical barriers (e.g., direct patient contact).
- **Reproducibility:** Ability for others to replicate the analysis.

## Final Selection: Pharmacovigilance of Semaglutide

**Topic:** Pharmacovigilance of Semaglutide (GLP-1 Receptor Agonist) — Post-marketing psychiatric adverse drug reaction analysis using FDA FAERS.

### Rationale for Selection

The evaluation matrix clearly identifies Pharmacovigilance and Adverse Drug Reactions (ADRs) as the most viable topic, scoring the highest across all criteria. Within this domain, **Semaglutide** was selected as the specific focus for several compelling reasons:

1.  **Dataset Accessibility:** The FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS) provides freely accessible tabular data. This ensures high feasibility for statistical analysis and reproducibility.
2.  **Clinical Relevance and Prescribing Trends:** Semaglutide, as a Glucagon-Like Peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist, represents one of the most prescribed new drug classes (2024-2026), particularly with its expanding indications for obesity and weight management alongside type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM).
3.  **Active Safety Investigations:** There are ongoing FDA safety investigations regarding potential psychiatric signals (e.g., suicidal ideation), as well as other conditions like Non-Arteritic Anterior Ischemic Optic Neuropathy (NAION) and gastroparesis. This makes the topic highly timely and impactful.
4.  **Methodological Precedents:** There is a robust body of published FAERS analyses, providing excellent methodological references for conducting disproportionality analysis and ensuring academic rigor.
