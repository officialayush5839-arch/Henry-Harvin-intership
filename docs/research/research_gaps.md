# Identified Research Gaps

A review of the current literature regarding Semaglutide and post-marketing safety data reveals several critical gaps in the existing body of knowledge. This research project is designed to address these deficiencies to provide a more rigorous understanding of potential psychiatric adverse drug reactions (ADRs).

## Key Research Gaps

1.  **Limited Comparative Disproportionality Studies:**
    Many existing analyses utilizing the FAERS database focus solely on a single drug of interest without employing an active comparator class. This approach makes it challenging to distinguish whether an observed increase in psychiatric reports is due to the drug itself or is a reflection of background rates within the patient population (e.g., patients with diabetes or obesity may have higher baseline rates of depression). A direct comparison with another class of medications used for similar indications (such as SGLT2 inhibitors) is essential for robust signal detection.

2.  **Under-studied Psychiatric ADRs:**
    The vast majority of post-marketing surveillance and literature concerning Semaglutide has focused heavily on gastrointestinal (GI) ADRs (e.g., gastroparesis, pancreatitis) and metabolic outcomes. Psychiatric ADRs, despite emerging case reports and regulatory attention, remain under-studied in large-scale systematic analyses relative to these other adverse event categories.

3.  **Lack of Temporal Trend Analysis:**
    The prescribing landscape for Semaglutide has evolved dramatically, particularly following the approval of Wegovy for weight management. There is a gap in understanding how the reporting signal for psychiatric ADRs has changed over time. Has the signal strengthened as prescribing expanded beyond the initial T2DM population? Temporal trend analysis is needed to identify patterns that might correlate with expanded indications or media attention (Weber effect/notoriety bias).

4.  **Limited Route-of-Administration Comparisons:**
    Semaglutide is unique among GLP-1 receptor agonists in that it is available in both subcutaneous injectable forms (Ozempic, Wegovy) and an oral formulation (Rybelsus). There is limited research comparing the reporting patterns of psychiatric adverse events across these different routes of administration to determine if pharmacokinetics or administration methods influence the ADR profile.

5.  **Disconnect Between FAERS Findings and Controlled Trial Evidence:**
    While spontaneous reporting systems are excellent for hypothesis generation, there are few studies that actively attempt to bridge the findings from FAERS disproportionality analyses back to the evidence (or lack thereof) from randomized controlled trials (RCTs). A comprehensive analysis needs to contextualize the signals generated from FAERS with the limitations of the original trial designs that may have precluded the detection of these events.
