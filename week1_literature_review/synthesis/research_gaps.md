# Research Gaps in Semaglutide Pharmacovigilance

## 1. Lack of Active Comparator Disproportionality Studies
A significant gap in the current literature is the over-reliance on the full FAERS database as the background comparator. Most disproportionality studies assess risk relative to all other drugs, rather than utilizing a clinically relevant active comparator (e.g., other GLP-1 RAs, SGLT2 inhibitors, or older anti-obesity medications). This limits the ability to determine if a signal is specific to semaglutide or indicative of a broader class effect.

## 2. Unclear Mechanisms for Psychiatric ADRs
While statistical signals for psychiatric events (depression, suicidality) have been identified, the mechanistic biological plausibility remains a profound gap. Although GLP-1 receptors are present in the central nervous system, the precise neurobiological pathways through which semaglutide might induce negative psychiatric outcomes are currently uncharacterized and require directed translational research.

## 3. Absence of Longitudinal Temporal Trend Analysis
The majority of FAERS analyses provide static snapshots of data over broad timeframes. There is a distinct lack of dynamic, temporal trend analyses tracking how safety signals evolve chronologically in response to expanding prescribing indications (from diabetes to weight loss) and shifting demographic profiles of users.

## 4. Limited Population-Specific Vulnerability Stratification
Current pharmacovigilance data largely treats the exposed population homogeneously. A critical gap is the lack of detailed stratification to identify vulnerable subgroups. Research must explore how adverse event risks differ based on age, sex, pre-existing psychiatric history, and the presence of concurrent medications (polypharmacy), moving towards personalized risk assessment.

## 5. Poorly Characterized Dose-Response Relationships
Semaglutide is prescribed across a wide dosage spectrum (e.g., 0.25mg for initial diabetes management to 2.4mg for weight loss). However, FAERS data often lacks reliable dosing information. Consequently, dose-response relationships for emerging safety signals remain poorly characterized, hindering optimal prescribing guidelines.

## 6. The Need for Prospective Validation
The most pressing research gap is the absence of dedicated, prospective active surveillance studies designed explicitly to validate the signals generated by FAERS. Retrospective database analyses are insufficient; prospective cohorts with rigorous baseline psychological assessments are urgently needed to definitively confirm or refute the psychiatric safety signals associated with semaglutide.
