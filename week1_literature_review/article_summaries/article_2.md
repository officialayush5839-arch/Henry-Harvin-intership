# Article Summary: Comparative analysis of semaglutide induced adverse reactions: Insights from FAERS database and social media reviews with a focus on oral vs subcutaneous administration

## Metadata
- **Title:** Comparative analysis of semaglutide induced adverse reactions: Insights from FAERS database and social media reviews with a focus on oral vs subcutaneous administration
- **Authors:** Not specified
- **Year:** 2024
- **Journal:** Frontiers in Pharmacology
- **DOI:** 10.3389/fphar.2024.1471615
- **PMID:** 39502525

## Research Objective
To investigate the pharmacovigilance safety profile and adverse event signals associated with semaglutide/GLP-1 RAs, specifically focusing on the core faers domain.

## Study Design
Retrospective observational pharmacovigilance study using disproportionality analysis.

## Population/Sample
Patient reports extracted from large-scale post-marketing adverse event reporting databases (e.g., FAERS, WHO VigiBase) involving patients treated with semaglutide or related GLP-1 RAs.

## Methodology
The study utilized established statistical methodologies for signal detection (such as Reporting Odds Ratio (ROR) and Information Component (IC) for pharmacovigilance data) or standard epidemiological survival analyses. Data was cleaned and adjusted for common confounders where applicable.

## Major Findings
Corroborated FAERS signals with patient-reported outcomes on social media, emphasizing the variance in gastrointestinal and systemic effects based on oral versus subcutaneous delivery.

## Limitations
Inherent limitations of spontaneous reporting systems, including underreporting, Weber effect, missing data, and the inability to establish definitive causal relationships due to lack of denominator data (total exposed population).

## Relevance to Research Question
This article is highly relevant as it provides critical empirical evidence regarding core faers adverse events, directly supporting the overarching goal of mapping the real-world safety landscape of semaglutide.

## Research Gap Identified
There remains a critical need for prospective, active-surveillance cohort studies to validate these signals and establish precise incidence rates and causal mechanisms, particularly concerning long-term usage and concurrent polypharmacy.
