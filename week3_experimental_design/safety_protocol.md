# Safety Protocol

**Disclaimer: This document outlines a SIMULATED/PROPOSED experimental design for educational and portfolio purposes. It is NOT an executed clinical trial.**

## General Safety & Biological Handling
- **BSL-1 Considerations:** The study involves routine phlebotomy and handling of standard blood samples, which pose minimal biohazard. Standard BSL-1 precautions will be maintained in all laboratory environments.
- **Phlebotomy Safety:** Standard venipuncture protocols will be followed to minimize discomfort, bruising, and infection risk.

## Psychiatric Emergency Protocol
Given the nature of the primary outcome, strict psychiatric safety nets are required:
- **PHQ-9 Trigger:** If a patient scores > 0 on Item 9 of the PHQ-9 (indicating any thoughts that they would be better off dead, or of hurting themselves), an automated alert is generated. This triggers an immediate, same-day clinical assessment by a licensed psychiatric professional.
- **C-SSRS Trigger:** Any positive response on the recent ideation or behavior items of the C-SSRS results in immediate safety protocol activation. This includes a psychiatric evaluation, potential unblinding of the patient's treatment arm, and emergency referral or hospitalization if deemed necessary by the assessing clinician.

## Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB)
- **Composition:** The independent DSMB will consist of an endocrinologist, a psychiatrist, a statistician, and a medical ethicist, none of whom are affiliated with the sponsor or clinical sites.
- **Meeting Schedule:** The DSMB will meet prior to trial initiation, at 25% enrollment, at the 50% interim analysis, at 75% completion, and ad hoc if safety signals arise.

## Adverse Event Reporting
- **SAE Procedures:** All Serious Adverse Events (SAEs)—including any psychiatric hospitalizations or suicide attempts—must be reported to the study sponsor and the IRB within 24 hours of site awareness.
- **Stopping Rules:** The trial will be halted early if the interim analysis crosses the pre-specified O'Brien-Fleming boundaries for harm (e.g., a statistically significant and clinically alarming excess of severe psychiatric events or suicidality in the Semaglutide arm compared to the SGLT2 inhibitor arm).
