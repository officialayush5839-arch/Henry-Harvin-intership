# Controls Rationale

**Disclaimer: This document outlines a SIMULATED/PROPOSED experimental design for educational and portfolio purposes. It is NOT an executed clinical trial.**

## Active Comparator Selection: SGLT2 Inhibitors
An active comparator, rather than a placebo, is employed in this study. The chosen comparator is the SGLT2 inhibitor class.
- **Similar Indication:** Both drug classes are widely prescribed for Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM) and have notable cardiovascular and renal benefits.
- **Different Mechanism:** SGLT2 inhibitors work primarily in the kidneys to increase glucose excretion, lacking the central nervous system (CNS) receptor activity observed with GLP-1 receptor agonists like Semaglutide.
- **Safety Profile:** SGLT2 inhibitors have a well-characterized safety profile with no established significant psychiatric or mood-altering side effects.

## Rationale Against Placebo
- **Ethical Concerns:** Withholding effective metabolic, glycemic, or weight management treatment from patients with clinical indications for 12 months is ethically problematic.
- **Placebo Effects on Mood:** Given that the outcome of interest relies heavily on subjective patient-reported mood, a pure placebo group may exhibit distinct psychological trajectories related to perceived lack of efficacy, which could confound comparisons.

## Control Measures and Statistical Adjustment
- **Propensity Score Matching:** To minimize selection bias, participants in observational arms (if incorporated alongside randomized arms) would be matched using propensity scores based on age, sex, baseline BMI, and baseline HbA1c.
- **Stratified Randomization:** Participants will be randomly assigned to treatments but stratified by key baseline psychiatric risk factors (e.g., baseline PHQ-9 score categories) to ensure equal distribution of inherent risk across both arms.
