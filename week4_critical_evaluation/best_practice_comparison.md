# Comparison of STEP 1 Trial against Best Practice Standards

To evaluate the methodological integrity and reporting quality of the STEP 1 trial, it must be assessed against recognized international standards. This document compares the trial's design and publication against elements of the CONSORT 2010 statement, the ICH-GCP guidelines, and the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 (RoB 2) tool.

## 1. CONSORT 2010 Statement (Reporting Standards)

The Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) guidelines ensure transparency and complete reporting of randomized controlled trials.

| CONSORT Element | Status in STEP 1 | Justification based on Publication |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Title and Abstract** (Identification as RCT) | Met | "Randomized" is explicitly stated in the trial design description in the abstract. |
| **Introduction** (Scientific background and rationale) | Met | Provided clear rationale regarding the need for highly effective pharmacotherapy for obesity and the basis for utilizing high-dose semaglutide. |
| **Methods: Trial Design** (Description of design, e.g., parallel, allocation ratio) | Met | Clearly stated as a Phase 3, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group trial with a 2:1 allocation ratio. |
| **Methods: Participants** (Eligibility criteria and settings) | Met | Detailed inclusion/exclusion criteria provided in supplementary material; multi-center, international setting clearly defined. |
| **Methods: Interventions** (Details of interventions for each group) | Met | Precise details on semaglutide dosing, the 16-week escalation protocol, matching placebo, and the standardized 500-kcal deficit/150-min exercise lifestyle intervention. |
| **Methods: Outcomes** (Primary and secondary endpoints defined) | Met | Co-primary endpoints (continuous and categorical weight loss) and hierarchical secondary endpoints rigorously defined. |
| **Methods: Sample Size** (Power calculation justification) | Met | Comprehensive sample size calculation provided, justifying N=1,950 based on assumed effect sizes and dropout rates. |
| **Methods: Randomization** (Sequence generation and concealment) | Met | Centralized Interactive Web-Response System (IWRS) used, ensuring strict allocation concealment. Stratification variables explicitly stated. |
| **Methods: Blinding** (Who was blinded and how) | Partially Met | Double-blinding achieved via visually identical placebo pens. However, no formal assessment of functional unblinding (due to GI side effects) was reported. |
| **Results: Participant Flow** (CONSORT diagram) | Met | A comprehensive participant flow diagram was included, detailing screening, randomization, dropouts, and analysis inclusion. |
| **Results: Baseline Data** (Table of demographic/clinical characteristics) | Met | Table 1 provided extensive baseline data, demonstrating balance between the randomized arms. |

## 2. ICH-GCP Guidelines (Execution Standards)

The International Council for Harmonisation of Technical Requirements for Pharmaceuticals for Human Use - Good Clinical Practice (ICH-GCP) governs the ethical and scientific quality standards for designing, conducting, and recording trials.

| ICH-GCP Principle | Status in STEP 1 | Justification based on Publication |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ethical Conduct and IRB Approval** | Met | Explicitly states the trial was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by relevant institutional review boards/ethics committees. |
| **Informed Consent** | Met | Stated that all participants provided written informed consent prior to initiating any trial-related procedures. |
| **Investigator Qualifications** | Assumed Met | Conducted across 129 specialized clinical sites; standard practice for Novo Nordisk Phase 3 trials requires rigorous site vetting. |
| **Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB)** | Met | The use of an independent, external data and safety monitoring board to oversee trial safety is documented. |
| **Protocol Adherence and Deviations** | Partially Met | While rigorous, the massive scale likely resulted in minor deviations, though the use of the "treatment policy" estimand robustly handled off-drug data. |

## 3. Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 (RoB 2) Tool (Methodological Integrity)

The Cochrane RoB 2 tool assesses the risk of bias in randomized trials across five specific domains.

| RoB 2 Domain | Assessed Risk | Justification based on Publication |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Domain 1: Risk of bias arising from the randomization process** | Low Risk | IWRS centralized randomization with concealed allocation guarantees a low risk of selection bias. Baseline characteristics were well-balanced. |
| **Domain 2: Risk of bias due to deviations from intended interventions** | Low Risk | Strict double-blinding minimized performance bias. The primary analysis utilized the treatment policy estimand (intention-to-treat), perfectly analyzing participants in the groups to which they were randomized, regardless of adherence. |
| **Domain 3: Risk of bias due to missing outcome data** | Low Risk | The trial maintained a very high completion rate (92.1%). Furthermore, missing data were handled using a conservative pattern-mixture model with multiple imputation, minimizing attrition bias. |
| **Domain 4: Risk of bias in measurement of the outcome** | Low Risk / Some Concerns | The primary endpoint (body weight) is an objective, automated measurement, highly resistant to detection bias (Low Risk). However, subjective secondary endpoints (like SF-36 physical functioning) could be biased by functional unblinding due to obvious side effects (Some Concerns). |
| **Domain 5: Risk of bias in selection of the reported result** | Low Risk | The trial protocol and statistical analysis plan were pre-registered on ClinicalTrials.gov. The published results perfectly align with the pre-specified primary and secondary endpoints, eliminating the risk of selective reporting. |
| **Overall Risk of Bias** | **Low Risk** | Overall, the STEP 1 trial demonstrates an exceptionally rigorous design with a low risk of bias for its objective primary endpoints. |
