# Selected Article Justification

**Article Title:** Once-Weekly Semaglutide in Adults with Overweight or Obesity (STEP 1 Trial)
**Journal:** New England Journal of Medicine (2021)

## Rationale for Selection

The STEP 1 (Semaglutide Treatment Effect in People with obesity) trial was carefully selected for critical evaluation in this portfolio for several compelling reasons:

### 1. Landmark Trial and Regulatory Impact
This specific randomized controlled trial (RCT) is a landmark study in the field of pharmacotherapy for obesity. The results from the STEP 1 trial directly supported the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approval of semaglutide 2.4 mg (Wegovy) for chronic weight management in adults. Evaluating a trial of such monumental regulatory significance provides critical insight into the standards required for contemporary drug approval.

### 2. Rigorous Study Design
The STEP 1 trial employs a robust, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design. This represents the gold standard for clinical research. Evaluating a trial with such a rigorous design allows for an in-depth exploration of advanced methodological concepts, including sophisticated blinding techniques, handling of missing data, and dual estimand approaches to efficacy analysis.

### 3. Direct Relevance to Portfolio Focus
The core focus of this research portfolio encompasses the safety, efficacy, and post-marketing surveillance of semaglutide. Analyzing the STEP 1 trial provides the foundational pre-approval clinical data. This establishes a baseline clinical trial safety profile that is essential for contextualizing the real-world safety signals (like psychiatric adverse events) analyzed via FAERS data in Week 2.

### 4. Rich Methodological Complexity
The study's methodology is highly complex, offering rich material for critical evaluation. Features such as the stratification during randomization, the implementation of a 16-week dose-escalation phase, the concurrent lifestyle intervention across both arms, and the use of the "treatment policy" and "trial product" estimands provide an excellent basis for methodological critique.

### 5. High-Impact Publication
Published in the *New England Journal of Medicine* (NEJM), one of the world's most prestigious and highly cited medical journals, this paper represents the pinnacle of peer-reviewed clinical research. Evaluating a paper of this caliber demonstrates an ability to critically engage with top-tier scientific literature.

### 6. Bridging Efficacy and Safety Contexts
While primarily powered for efficacy (weight loss), the trial extensively reports on safety outcomes. By critically reviewing the inclusion/exclusion criteria (such as the exclusion of patients with a recent psychiatric history) and the sample size limitations regarding rare adverse events, we can directly link the inherent limitations of RCTs to the necessity of rigorous post-marketing pharmacovigilance. This bridging directly supports the narrative arc of the entire portfolio.
